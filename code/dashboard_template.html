Data & Method

Every record traces to a public source; there is no synthetic data.

Pipeline

Pipeline

1 · CollectRBI holiday matrix scraped for 34 regional offices, 2022–2026 (60 archived pages); Census 2011 C-01; 78 economic figures checked on their source pages.
2 · Identify101 festivals matched by pattern; each festival's tradition and home community taken from its Wikipedia infobox or a Government page.
3 · CorrectSunday correction (RBI lists no Sundays) and shared-date evidence scoring, so no religion, state or festival is under-counted.
4 · AnalyseDescriptive statistics, 9 hypothesis tests, K-Means, Random Forest and trade regression with leave-one-out and 2026 hold-out checks.

Sources

Reserve Bank of India — Holiday Matrix (rbi.org.in), 2022–2026
Census of India 2011 — Table C-01, religious community (India and Andhra Pradesh district tables)
DoPT — compulsory Central Government holidays, O.M. F.No.12/2/2023-JCA (3 July 2025)
Wikipedia / State Government pages — tradition and home community of each festival
CAIT, IBEF, AIR, State tourism departments and others — festive trade and footfall figures
DataMeet — state boundaries (CC BY 2.5 IN)

Limits

Only festivals with a bank holiday are counted; restricted and district-level holidays are not.
Union territories without an RBI office are not covered.
Punjab, Haryana and Chandigarh share one RBI office and appear as one unit.
Single-year views show dates as listed by RBI (no Sunday correction); the 2022–26 average is corrected.
Trade figures are published estimates, mostly from CAIT; the regression has 15 training records.
